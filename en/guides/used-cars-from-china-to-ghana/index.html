Age and condition rules

Ghana applies an age-based penalty scale to used vehicle imports, which means an older unit can attract a materially higher duty rate than a newer one of the same value. Confirm the current scale for your model year with a licensed clearing agent before you choose a vehicle, because the scale is revised periodically and it changes the total landed cost more than the vehicle price does.

Ports and routing

Most vehicle imports arrive at Tema, with Takoradi used for the western corridor. RORO is the usual choice for a single unit; container shipping is used when you consolidate several vehicles or when the unit is not driveable. Ask the supplier which service they actually booked, because RORO and container have different terminal handling charges at destination.

Documents you should receive

  • Commercial invoice matching the declared value
  • Bill of Lading with the consignee details you specified
  • Certificate of Origin
  • Export declaration from China
  • Payment receipt

Check that the consignee name on the Bill of Lading is exactly the entity that will clear the vehicle. Correcting it after the vessel sails is slow and expensive.

Left-hand drive and specification

Ghana drives on the right, so left-hand drive is required. Confirm LHD in writing. Also confirm whether the unit has a catalytic converter and the emission standard it meets, because both can affect clearance.

Budget beyond the vehicle price

Plan for duty and levies, clearing agent fees, terminal charges, port storage if clearance is slow, and inland transport to your city. Importers who budget only the CIF number are the ones who end up with a vehicle sitting at the port accruing storage charges.
