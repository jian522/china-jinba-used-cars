What actually matters on African roads

Three things decide whether an SUV survives well: ground clearance, suspension durability and parts availability. A premium badge with 160mm of clearance and no local parts network is a worse commercial choice than a modest Chinese SUV with 190mm of clearance and a dealer two hours away. Buyers who ignore this end up importing vehicles that are expensive to keep on the road.

Models worth comparing

  • Haval H6 - the most established Chinese SUV in African markets, with the widest parts and service coverage. Ride comfort is good; early dual-clutch examples need a careful test drive.
  • Changan CS75 / CS55 - typically cheaper than Haval for similar size, with simpler mechanicals on the CS55.
  • Geely Boyue / Atlas - Volvo-derived platform, strong safety structure, good highway behaviour.
  • GAC GS4 / GS8 - joint-venture build quality, growing parts presence.
  • BYD Song Plus - plug-in hybrid or EV, low running cost, but only where charging or hybrid service exists.

Petrol, hybrid or electric

Petrol remains the safest choice where fuel quality varies and workshop familiarity is limited. Hybrids make sense for high-mileage urban fleets. Full EVs make sense only where charging is reliable and where the buyer understands battery degradation. Do not import an EV into a market with no service capability and expect the resale value to hold.

Specification checks before ordering

  • Left-hand drive, confirmed in writing
  • Ground clearance and tyre size for the roads it will actually use
  • Whether the engine tolerates the local fuel octane and sulphur level
  • Availability of filters, brake pads and suspension bushes in the destination market
