Home / Buying guides / How to Import Used Cars from China to Kenya: FOB vs CIF Explained

Second hand cars from China — especially used BYD EVs — are among the fastest-growing import categories for Kenyan dealers. This guide breaks down the real cost structure of FOB Shenzhen and CIF Mombasa shipments, the export documents you must receive, and how to verify pre-inspected stock before wiring any payment.

FOB Shenzhen vs CIF Mombasa: what each price includes. FOB covers the vehicle, 168-point pre-shipment inspection, export customs clearance in China and loading at Shekou or Yantian. You arrange ocean freight, marine insurance and Kenyan import charges. CIF Mombasa adds freight and insurance to a single number — the simplest way to plan landed cost — while duty (varies by vehicle age and value), IDF, RDL and Mombasa port handling stay outside the quote either way.

The export document checklist. Your clearing agent needs: the Bill of Lading (original or seaway waybill), the commercial invoice and packing list, the export declaration, and the Certificate of Inspection (COI) required under KEBS pre-export verification of conformity. EVs additionally carry battery handling documentation aligned with UN 3480 sea-transport rules.

168-point pre-shipment inspection: what we check. Structure and accident history, verified odometer against service records, battery health testing for EVs, mechanical and electronics operation, interior/exterior condition with a dated photo set. You may appoint an independent inspector in Shenzhen before the container is stuffed.

Container consolidation for bulk shipment. A 40ft container takes 4–6 SUVs or 8–10 compact cars. Most Kenyan dealers mix 2–4 BYD EVs with 2–4 petrol SUVs to serve both Nairobi/E-mombasa-city buyers and upcountry retail. Sharing freight across a full container typically cuts per-unit shipping cost by 30–45% versus single-unit consolidations.

Payment safety for first-time importers. Pay only to the contracted company account, insist on a written quotation that names the stock number, and treat any last-minute beneficiary change as a stop signal. Standard terms: 30% deposit to reserve units, 70% balance against a copy of the Bill of Lading.

Typical timeline. Quotation same day → deposit and unit reservation → inspection and container stuffing (3–7 days) → sail from Shekou → 25–32 days port to port → discharge and clearing at Mombasa. Ask for this week's CIF Mombasa rate list — it moves with the freight market.
