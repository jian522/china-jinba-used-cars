The timeline has five stages

Buyers usually quote the ocean leg and forget the rest. A realistic plan covers: vehicle release and inspection, export customs, ocean transit, destination clearance, and inland delivery. Only the middle stage is fixed by the shipping line.

  • Release and inspection - 2 to 7 days, longer if a repair or part is needed
  • Export customs and loading - 3 to 7 days
  • Ocean transit - 25 to 40 days to West Africa, 20 to 30 days to East Africa, depending on rotation
  • Destination clearance - 5 to 20 days, driven by documentation quality and duty payment speed
  • Inland delivery - 1 to 10 days

Why the ocean leg varies so much

RORO vessels follow a rotation, so a port that is served weekly is very different from one served monthly. Transhipment adds days and adds handling risk. If timing matters, ask for the specific vessel and the estimated arrival at your port, and accept that the estimate moves.

Where delays actually come from

In our experience the delays that hurt are not at sea. They come from a consignee name that does not match on the Bill of Lading, a duty payment that waits for a bank transfer, or an inspection that finds an undeclared item. Every one of those is preventable before the vessel sails.

Planning backwards from a date

If you need the vehicle on the ground by a certain date, work backwards: inland delivery, clearance, ocean transit, loading, release. Add two weeks of buffer. A supplier who promises a fixed arrival date without buffer is guessing.
