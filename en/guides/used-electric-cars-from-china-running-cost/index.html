The arithmetic that matters

An EV's advantage is entirely in the running cost, so the comparison only works if you use real local prices. Take annual distance, local electricity price per kWh, local petrol price, and the vehicle's consumption. Then add maintenance, which is lower on an EV mainly because there is no oil, fewer filters and regenerative braking that extends pad life.

Illustrative comparison

At 30,000 km per year, a compact electric sedan consuming around 15 kWh per 100 km uses roughly 4,500 kWh annually. At $0.10 per kWh that is about $450. A petrol equivalent at 7 L per 100 km uses about 2,100 litres; at $1.20 per litre that is about $2,520. The gap is real, but only if the electricity price and charging access are real.

Battery health is the whole valuation

A used EV without a battery state-of-health report is a speculative purchase. Ask for a state-of-health percentage from a diagnostic session, the cell balance summary, and the number of fast-charge cycles where available. A pack at 85% of original capacity behaves very differently from one at 70%, and replacement cost can exceed the vehicle's value.

When an EV import makes sense

  • Urban or peri-urban fleet with predictable daily distance
  • Reliable charging at a depot, not dependence on public charging
  • Local technician capability for high-voltage systems
  • Buyer who understands degradation and resale risk

Outside those conditions, a petrol or hybrid unit is the more defensible commercial decision.
