How to Import Used Cars to Southeast Asia from China

Southeast Asia is emerging as a major market for Chinese used cars. Learn about import procedures for Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia, Philippines, and Indonesia.

January 1, 2026·14 min read

The Growing Chinese Car Presence in Southeast Asia

Southeast Asia has become a strategic market for Chinese automakers. With improved quality and competitive pricing, Chinese used cars are gaining traction across the region. The ASEAN framework facilitates trade, making it easier to import vehicles between member states.

Country-by-Country Guide

Vietnam 🇻🇳

Vietnam has become one of the largest importers of Chinese vehicles. Key ports: Hai Phong, Ho Chi Minh City. Chinese brands like Chery, Geely, and MG have strong presence. Import duty for used cars: 50-70% depending on age and engine size.

Thailand 🇹🇭

Thailand is the automotive hub of Southeast Asia. While known for Japanese brands, Chinese EVs are gaining ground. Bangkok port handles most imports. Duty ranges from 20-80% depending on vehicle type.

Malaysia 🇲🇾

Malaysia has specific standards (SIRIM) for vehicle imports. Chinese brands like Geely, Chery, and GAC are well-established. Port of Klang handles imports. Used vehicle duty: 30-105% depending on specifications.

Philippines 🇵🇭

Manila and Cebu ports handle vehicle imports. Chinese brands are growing in popularity. Import duty for used cars: 40% (with additional taxes). Age limit: 5 years for most vehicles.

Indonesia 🇮🇩

Indonesia has strict regulations but growing demand. Surabaya and Jakarta ports are main entry points. Chinese EVs benefit from government incentives. Used vehicle import restrictions apply.

ASEAN Trade Benefits

The ASEAN Complete Car concept allows for reduced duties on vehicles meeting regional standards. Key benefits:

  • Reduced import duties for compliant vehicles
  • Harmonized safety and emission standards
  • Easier customs clearance between member states
  • Growing regional supply chain integration

Import Tips

  1. Check local regulations: Each country has different requirements
  2. Verify compatibility: Ensure vehicles meet local standards
  3. Consider right-hand drive: Malaysia and Philippines require RHD
  4. Plan for certification: Some countries require local testing
  5. Build local partnerships: Work with established dealers
